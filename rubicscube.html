Beginner Solve

  1. Make a cross on one side, then fill in the corners (the first step should be intuitive)
  2. With the solved side towards the bottom, bring find an edge for the middle layer and bring it above its correct color if it has to go the...
    ...left: U' L' U L U F U' F'
    ...right: U R U' R' U' F' U F
  3. Position the corners: With the unsolved side up you can switch the two corners facing you with L' U' L F U F' L' U L U2. If you want to switch both the front two corners and the back two corners: do L' U' L F U2 F' L' U L
  4. Orient the corners: Rotate all corners except of the front left clockwise with R U R' U R U2 R' U2
    or all corners exept the front right counter clockwise with L' U' L U' L' U2' L U2'
  5. Position the edges: Rotate every corner exept the one facing you counterclockwise with Mu U' Md U' U' Mu U' Md
    or counterclockwise with Mu U Md U U Mu U Md>
  6. Orient the edges: Turn the left and right edge with R' Ml R2 Mr Mr R' U2 R Mr Mr R2' Mr R U2
    and/or turn the front and the left edge F Ml R2 Mr2 R' U2 R Mr2 R2' Mr R U2 R' F'

Blindfolded Solve

For a blindfolded solve only two algorithms are needed.
  1. The Edge Swap: Swaps UR (buffer) with UL (target) : [sexy] R' F R2 U' [rev. sexy] R' F'
  2. The Corner Swap: Swaps LBU (buffer) with DFR (target) : R U' [rev. sexy] R' F' [sexy] R' F R

Basic Algorythms

The Notation

  • A single letter by itself refers to a clockwise face rotation in 90 degrees. F, R, U, L, B, D - front, right, upfacing side, left, back side, downfacing side
  • A letter followed by an apostrophe means to turn that face counterclockwise 90 degrees.
  • A letter with the number 2 after it marks a double turn.
Name Moves Discription
Sune R U R' U R U2 R' Sune is an OLL algorithm, which means it orients the last layer. It is part of a special subcategory called OCLL, which means that it only orients the corners (is used when all edges are oriented). It was proposed by Lars Petrus in his Petrus method.
Anti-Sune R U2 R' U' R U' R' As referenced by the name, Anti-Sune is the opposite of Sune. It is still an OCLL, but the algorithm is mirrored. It was also coined by Petrus in the method of the same name.
Sledgehammer R' F R F' This is a trigger that is used in a lot of algorithms, and in F2L. It also has a much lesser-known reverse, hedgeslammer. If repeated 6 times, it will bring the cube back to its previous state.
Sexy Move R U R' U' This is another trigger that is heavily used in almost everything. You can find it in F2L, OLL, and PLL and, if repeated 6 times on a cube, will bring it back to the same state it was in before.
Reverse Sexy U R U' R' This is, as the name implies, the reverse of sexy. It is less used, but is still quite prominent in F2L, where the triple sexy is frequently replaced with triple reverse sexy as it is said to be quicker. Either way, if repeated 6 times it will bring the cube back to its original state, as with most 6 move triggers.
U Perms Ua: M2 U M U2 M' U M2
Ub: M2 U' M U2 M' U' M2 		This is a PLL (Position Last Layer) algorithm. There are 2 variants, the Ua and Ub perms. They are used when all the corners are permuted and there are 3 edges to permute in a triangular fashion. Doing either one 3 times will bring the cube back to its original state and executing either one once will make the case that the other one solves.
T Perm R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R' U' R U R' F' The T perm is perhaps the most well-known PLL algorithm, with its only competition being the U perms (above) and the J perms (below). It is used to permute 2 opposite edges and two adjacent corners, and the shape of those pieces to permute when viewed from above makes a T, hence the name.
J Perms Ja: R' U L' U2 R U' R' U2 R L or L' U' L F L' U' L U L F' L2' U L U
Jb: R U R' F' R U R' U' R' F R2 U' R' U' 		These are 2 PLL algorithms that permute 2 adjacent edges and 2 adjacent corners. It is recognisable by the sheer number of blocks it has. There is one solved line, and 2 unsolved blocks. The Jb tends to be the faster one, as it is an RUF algorithm, but the Ja – being either an RUL or LUF algorithm – can also be very fast with practice.
H Perm M2' U' M2' U2' M2' U' M2' The H perm is a PLL algorithm that swaps 2 sets of opposite edges. All the corners are already solved. The directions of the U turns can be switched.
Key (OLL 33) R U R' U' R' F R F' This OLL looks like a T when viewed from the top. It is not the only OLL to look like this but it is recognizable from the two opposite facing blocks that the unoriented pieces make.
Bottlecap (OLL 51) f ( R U R' U' ) ( R U R' U' ) f' or F U R U' R' U R U' R' F' This OLL is a very famous one, and a good example of an OLL that contains the ‘sexy' trigger. It is a Line case, and it has 2 opposing edge and corner blocks, along with two adjacent corners that make headlights.
T (OLL 45) F R U R' U' F' This OLL is probably one of the most famous out of all the full algorithms. It is 6 moves long, and the other T shaped algorithm in addition to Key, above. The main body of the algorithm is the sexy trigger.
Checkerboard M2 E2 S2 or R2 L2 U2 D2 F2 B2 This is a pattern but has a famous algorithm to make it. This is quite an intuitive algorithm, and one that many beginner cubers will be taught or figure out or see done.
Z Perm M' U' M2' U' M2' U' M' U2 M2' U This permutation is a PLL algorithm. It switches two sets of adjacent edges. All the corners are already solved when this algorithm is used. It is used in every method that forces an EPLL (a PLL of only the edges, in other words, all corners are pre-solved) including Petrus.
